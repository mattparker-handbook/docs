When budgeting for a home purchase in British Columbia, the purchase price is only the starting point. **Closing costs** — the fees and taxes due at or before the completion date — typically range from **2% to 5% of the purchase price** for most BC buyers. On a $800,000 home, that's $16,000 to $40,000 in additional expenses beyond your down payment. Failing to plan for these costs is one of the most common financial surprises for first-time buyers. This guide breaks down every significant closing cost you should anticipate.

<Tip>
  As a general rule, budget a **1–2% buffer** on top of your estimated closing costs. Unexpected items — a strata document review, a title search complication, or a last-minute survey certificate — can add up quickly and are difficult to predict in advance.
</Tip>

## Detailed Cost Breakdown

### Property Transfer Tax (PTT)

Property Transfer Tax is a **provincial tax** levied on every registered transfer of real property in BC. It is one of the largest closing costs for most buyers and is due on the completion date.

The standard PTT rate structure is:
- **1%** on the first $200,000 of fair market value
- **2%** on the portion from $200,001 to $2,000,000
- **3%** on the portion from $2,000,001 to $3,000,000
- **5%** on the portion over $3,000,000 (residential properties)

**Example:** For a $900,000 purchase: ($200,000 × 1%) + ($700,000 × 2%) = $2,000 + $14,000 = **$16,000 PTT**.

Exemptions exist for **first-time home buyers** (properties up to $835,000) and **newly built homes** (properties up to $1,100,000). See our [Property Transfer Tax guide](/client-guide/property-transfer-tax) for full details on exemptions and eligibility.

### Legal / Notary Fees

In BC, real estate transactions are completed by either a **lawyer** or a **notary public**. They handle the title transfer, mortgage registration, fund disbursement, and preparation of all closing documents.

**What's included:**
- Review of the Contract of Purchase and Sale
- Title search and registration of title transfer
- Registration of the new mortgage
- Preparation of the statement of adjustments
- Liaising with the seller's lawyer and your lender

**Disbursements** (additional charges within this range) cover land title registration fees, courier costs, and document search fees. Always request a written estimate from your legal professional before engagement.

---

### Home Inspection

A professional home inspection is **strongly recommended** for all resale properties. An inspector evaluates the structural and mechanical condition of the home — roof, foundation, plumbing, electrical, HVAC — and provides a written report.

- Detached homes: typically **$500–$700**
- Condos/strata units: typically **$400–$550** (inspectors may not access all common areas)
- Older homes or larger properties may cost more

While not a mandatory closing cost, the inspection is typically paid at the time of the inspection — before subjects are removed — and should be factored into your early-purchase budget.

---

### Title Insurance

Title insurance protects you and your lender against losses arising from **title defects**, survey issues, encroachments, liens, or fraud that may not be discovered during the standard title search. It is a **one-time premium** (not an annual fee) paid at closing.

Most BC lawyers and notaries automatically include title insurance as part of closing. Common providers include FCT (First Canadian Title) and Stewart Title. Even in cases where no issues are found during a title search, title insurance provides ongoing protection against problems discovered after the fact.

---

### CMHC Mortgage Default Insurance Premium

If your down payment is **less than 20%** of the purchase price, your mortgage must be insured against default by one of three approved mortgage insurers: **CMHC**, **Sagen**, or **Canada Guaranty**. This protects the lender — not the borrower — but the premium is paid by the borrower.

| Down Payment | Insurance Premium (% of mortgage) |
|---|---|
| 5% – 9.99% | 4.00% |
| 10% – 14.99% | 3.10% |
| 15% – 19.99% | 2.80% |
| 20%+ | No insurance required |

The premium is **added to your mortgage balance** and amortized over the life of the loan — it is not typically a direct out-of-pocket cost at closing. However, **provincial sales tax on the premium** may apply in some provinces (BC does not currently charge PST on CMHC premiums, but confirm this with your broker).

**Example:** On an $800,000 purchase with 5% down ($40,000), the mortgage is $760,000. The CMHC premium is 4.0% × $760,000 = **$30,400**, added to the mortgage.

---

### Appraisal Fee

Lenders may require an independent appraisal to confirm the property's market value supports the purchase price and mortgage amount. Appraisals are conducted by a **Certified Residential Appraiser (CRA)**.

- Cost: typically **$300–$600** for a standard residential property
- Some lenders waive the appraisal fee or absorb the cost as part of their mortgage offer
- For high-value, unique, or rural properties, the cost may be higher

If an appraisal comes in **below the purchase price**, this can affect your mortgage approval — the lender will calculate the loan-to-value ratio based on the appraised value, not the purchase price.

---

### Strata Move-In / Move-Out Fee

If you are purchasing a **strata property** (condominium or townhome), the strata corporation may charge a move-in fee to cover elevator booking, common area protection, and administrative costs. This fee varies by strata and is set by the strata council.

- Typical range: **$0 to $500**
- Check the strata bylaws and the **Form B (Information Certificate)** for the exact fee
- This fee is separate from any strata documents review fee your lawyer may charge

---

### Property Tax Adjustment

At closing, property taxes are **pro-rated between buyer and seller** to the completion date. If the seller has already paid property taxes for the full year, you will owe them a credit for your portion. If taxes haven't yet been paid, you'll be responsible from the completion date forward.

The exact amount depends on the annual property tax bill and the completion date. Your notary or lawyer will calculate this adjustment in the **Statement of Adjustments**.

---

### Home Insurance

Your lender will require proof of **home (property) insurance** effective on the completion date before releasing mortgage funds. For strata properties, your unit requires contents and personal liability coverage (the strata corporation covers the building envelope separately under the master strata policy).

Annual premiums vary by property type, age, location, and coverage level. Budget **$1,000–$2,500 per year** for a detached home in BC; strata unit coverage is typically less expensive.

## New Construction: GST Applies

<Note>
  If you are purchasing a **newly built home** from a builder, **GST (5% of the purchase price)** applies to the transaction. This is in addition to PTT (though new builds may qualify for the Newly Built Home PTT Exemption). A **GST/HST New Housing Rebate** is available for primary residences where the purchase price is under $450,000, which can reduce the net GST payable. Ensure your purchase contract specifies whether the listed price is GST-inclusive or GST-exclusive — this significantly affects your total cost. See our [First-Time Buyer Programs guide](/client-guide/first-time-buyer-programs) for rebate details.
</Note>

## Closing Cost Checklist

Before your completion date, ensure you have funds available for:

- [ ] Property Transfer Tax (unless exempt)
- [ ] Legal / notary fees and disbursements
- [ ] Home inspection (paid before subjects removed)
- [ ] Title insurance (usually included in legal fees)
- [ ] Appraisal fee (if not waived by lender)
- [ ] Strata move-in fee (if applicable)
- [ ] Property tax adjustment (net credit or debit)
- [ ] First-year home insurance premium
- [ ] 1–2% buffer for unexpected costs

<Tip>
  Your lawyer or notary will provide a **Statement of Adjustments** a few days before closing with the precise figures for each item. Review this carefully and ensure your down payment funds plus closing costs are in a readily accessible account — not locked in a GIC or investment that requires advance notice to redeem.
</Tip>
