## The Four Stages of Getting a Mortgage in BC

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pre-Approval">
    Before you start shopping for a home, a mortgage pre-approval establishes how much you can borrow, locks in a rate for a set period, and gives sellers confidence that you're a serious buyer. This stage involves a financial assessment, document collection, and a credit review with your broker.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Property Search & Offer">
    Armed with your pre-approval, you work with your realtor to find a property within your budget. When you find the right home, your realtor submits an offer — typically subject to financing — which gives you a defined window to confirm your mortgage before the deal becomes firm.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Financing Confirmation">
    Once your offer is accepted, your mortgage broker submits a full application to the lender. The lender reviews your documents, orders an appraisal if required, and issues a commitment letter. You then remove your financing subject by the agreed deadline, making the purchase firm.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Closing">
    In the final stage, your lawyer or notary reviews and registers the title transfer, the lender advances mortgage funds, and all closing costs are paid. On completion day, the keys are yours. Your broker remains available to help you understand your new mortgage terms and plan for renewal.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  British Columbia has several mortgage and property rules that don't apply in other provinces. These include the **Property Transfer Tax (PTT)**, BC's **First-Time Home Buyers' Program** PTT exemption, and the federal **First Home Savings Account (FHSA)** — a registered account that lets eligible first-time buyers save up to $40,000 tax-free toward a home purchase. This guide highlights BC-specific rules throughout so you're never caught off guard.
</Note>
