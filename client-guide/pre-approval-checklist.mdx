A mortgage pre-approval does more than tell you how much you can borrow — it positions you as a credible buyer in a competitive BC market, locks in an interest rate for up to 120 days, and surfaces any credit or documentation issues before you're under contract. To issue a pre-approval, your lender needs to verify your identity, income, employment stability, and available assets. The documents required vary based on how you earn your income. Use the tabs below to find your specific checklist.

<Note>
  Start gathering your documents as early as possible — ideally before your first broker meeting. Incomplete document packages are the single most common cause of delays at the pre-approval and financing stages. Having everything ready upfront can reduce your overall timeline by several business days.
</Note>

## Income & Employment Documents

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Employed">
    If you receive a regular paycheque from an employer (full-time, part-time, or contract with a T4), use this checklist.

    **Identity**
    - **Government-issued photo ID** — passport, driver's licence, or provincial ID card (two pieces required)
    - **SIN (Social Insurance Number)** — for credit bureau authorization

    **Employment Confirmation**
    - **Letter of employment** — on company letterhead, dated within 30 days, stating your position, start date, employment type (permanent/contract), and annual salary or hourly wage
    - **Most recent pay stub** — showing year-to-date earnings and deductions

    **Income History**
    - **T4 slips** — most recent two years
    - **Federal Notice of Assessment (NOA)** — most recent two years (CRA My Account is the fastest source)

    **If you earn variable income (overtime, bonuses, commissions)**
    - **T4 slips for the past two years** — lenders average variable income over 24 months
    - **Most recent pay stub** showing year-to-date variable earnings
    - **Employment letter** confirming base salary and that variable pay is part of your compensation structure

    **Assets & Banking**
    - **Bank statements** — most recent 90 days for all accounts used for your down payment
    - **Investment or RRSP statements** — most recent statement confirming account balance
    - **Gift letter** — if any portion of your down payment is a gift (see Down Payment section below)
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Self-Employed">
    If you operate a business, work as a sole proprietor, or are an incorporated business owner drawing income, lenders require additional documentation to establish two-year income history.

    **Identity**
    - **Government-issued photo ID** — two pieces required
    - **SIN (Social Insurance Number)** — for credit bureau authorization

    **Business Confirmation**
    - **Business registration or Articles of Incorporation** — confirming your business name and registration date
    - **GST/HST registration number** (if applicable)
    - **Accountant's letter** — confirming your ownership percentage and the financial health of the business (not required by all lenders, but strengthens the file)

    **Personal Income Verification**
    - **Federal Notice of Assessment (NOA)** — most recent two years (line 15000 is the key income figure lenders use)
    - **T1 General tax returns** — most recent two years, all pages
    - **T4 and/or T5 slips** — most recent two years if you pay yourself a salary and/or dividends

    **Business Financials**
    - **Accountant-prepared financial statements** — most recent two years (income statement and balance sheet); must be prepared by a CPA
    - **Corporate tax returns (T2)** — most recent two years (for incorporated businesses)

    **Assets & Banking**
    - **Personal bank statements** — most recent 90 days for all accounts contributing to the down payment
    - **Business bank statements** — most recent 90 days (some lenders require these to verify business cash flow)
    - **Investment or RRSP statements** — most recent statement

    **Important note for self-employed borrowers:** Lenders typically average your net income over the most recent two tax years. If your income has declined in year two, some lenders will use the lower figure. Your broker can identify lenders whose policies are most favourable to your specific income structure.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Retired / Investment Income">
    If your primary income comes from pensions, investments, rental income, or government benefits, use this checklist. Lenders treat each source differently.

    **Identity**
    - **Government-issued photo ID** — two pieces required
    - **SIN (Social Insurance Number)** — for credit bureau authorization

    **Pension & Government Benefits**
    - **CPP Statement of Estimated Benefits** or most recent CPP payment confirmation
    - **OAS confirmation letter** or benefit statement
    - **Employer pension letter** — confirming monthly benefit amount, commencement date, and whether the pension is indexed or has a survivor benefit
    - **T4A slips** — most recent two years, for all pension and benefit income

    **Investment & Dividend Income**
    - **Most recent T1 General return** — all pages, to confirm investment income reported (lines 12000, 12100, 12200)
    - **Federal NOA** — most recent two years
    - **Investment account statements** — most recent 90 days showing account balances, and most recent annual statement showing income distributions
    - **T3 and T5 slips** — most recent two years

    **Rental Income**
    - **Signed lease agreement(s)** for all rental properties
    - **Most recent T1 General return** — Schedule showing rental income and expenses (Form T776)
    - **Federal NOA** — most recent two years
    - **Mortgage statement(s)** for each rental property showing current balance and monthly payment

    **Assets & Banking**
    - **Bank statements** — most recent 90 days for all accounts used for the down payment
    - **Non-registered and registered investment account statements** — most recent quarterly or annual statement
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Down Payment Documentation

Lenders require a clear paper trail showing where your down payment funds originated. The required documentation depends on the source of your funds.

- **Personal savings** — Bank statements for the most recent 90 days showing the funds on deposit and their accumulation over time. Sudden large deposits require explanation.
- **RRSP (Home Buyers' Plan)** — RRSP account statement showing current balance; confirmation that the account has been open for at least 90 days (required for HBP withdrawal eligibility); completed T1036 form once withdrawal is made.
- **Gift from a family member** — Signed gift letter confirming the donor's name and relationship to you, the gift amount, the fact that no repayment is required, and the donor's bank statement showing the funds exist. Gifted down payments are subject to lender-specific policies on eligible donors.
- **Proceeds from the sale of another property** — Accepted offer or firm sale agreement showing net proceeds, and a bridge financing confirmation if closing dates don't align.
- **Company equity or business funds** — Requires additional documentation; speak with your broker before using business funds as this has specific tax and lender implications.
- **Foreign-sourced funds** — Funds held outside Canada require 90 days of foreign bank statements translated into English or French and may be subject to anti-money laundering verification. Processing times are longer — disclose this to your broker early.

## Property-Related Documents (At Offer Stage)

Once your offer is accepted, you'll need to provide the following to advance to a full mortgage application:

- **Accepted purchase and sale agreement** — fully executed, all pages including subjects, schedules, and any amendments
- **MLS listing** — property address, list price, and features (broker can pull this; a copy from your realtor is also acceptable)
- **Property tax assessment** — current year's BC Assessment notice (your realtor can usually provide this)
- **Strata documents** — if purchasing a condo, townhouse, or any strata-titled property: Form B Information Certificate, current budget, most recent depreciation report, meeting minutes for the past two years, and strata bylaws
- **Well and septic documentation** — for rural or acreage properties, potability test results and septic inspection report may be required by the lender

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How long is a pre-approval valid in BC?">
    Most mortgage pre-approvals in BC include a **rate hold of 90 to 120 days**, depending on the lender. During this period, the lender commits to honouring the rate quoted as long as your financial situation remains unchanged. If you haven't found a property before the pre-approval expires, your broker can renew it — typically requiring updated documents such as a new pay stub and a refreshed credit check. Note that a pre-approval does not guarantee final approval; the specific property you purchase must also satisfy the lender's criteria.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does pre-approval guarantee a mortgage?">
    No — a pre-approval is a conditional commitment based on your financial profile at a point in time, but it is **not a guarantee of final mortgage approval**. Final approval depends on the specific property (its appraised value, title status, and property type), confirmation that your income and employment have not changed, and satisfying any conditions the lender identifies during underwriting. This is why purchase offers in BC typically include a subject-to-financing clause — it protects you if the lender's final review raises concerns the pre-approval didn't anticipate.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Will applying for a pre-approval hurt my credit score?">
    It depends on the stage. When your broker initially reviews your situation, they typically run a **soft credit inquiry** (also called a soft pull), which does not affect your credit score and is not visible to other lenders. When you proceed to a formal pre-approval, the lender performs a **hard credit inquiry**, which is recorded on your credit file and may cause a minor, temporary dip in your score (usually 5–10 points). Multiple mortgage hard inquiries within a 14–45 day window are generally treated as a single inquiry by the credit bureaus under a "rate shopping" allowance — so having your broker submit to two or three lenders simultaneously has less impact than it might appear. Your broker will discuss the credit pull process with you before anything is submitted.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
