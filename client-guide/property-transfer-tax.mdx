**Property Transfer Tax (PTT)** is a provincial tax payable to the Government of British Columbia on **every registered transfer of real property** — including purchases, gifts, and certain changes in ownership. It is due on the **completion date** and is one of the largest closing costs for BC home buyers. PTT is calculated based on the **fair market value** of the property at the time of transfer (which is typically the purchase price). Understanding how PTT is calculated — and which exemptions you may qualify for — can save you tens of thousands of dollars.

<Note>
  PTT is administered by the BC Ministry of Finance. Your lawyer or notary will complete the **Property Transfer Tax Return** (including Schedule A) on your behalf at closing. You should retain all records related to your purchase, including documents supporting any exemption claimed.
</Note>

## PTT Rate Structure

PTT is calculated on a **tiered (marginal) rate** basis — similar to income tax brackets — applied to the fair market value of the property.

| Fair Market Value | PTT Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to $200,000 | 1% |
| $200,001 – $2,000,000 | 2% |
| $2,000,001 – $3,000,000 | 3% |
| Over $3,000,000 | 5% (residential properties) |

### Example Calculation — $900,000 Home

| Value Bracket | Amount in Bracket | Rate | Tax Payable |
|---|---|---|---|
| First $200,000 | $200,000 | 1% | $2,000 |
| $200,001 – $900,000 | $700,000 | 2% | $14,000 |
| **Total PTT** | | | **$16,000** |

On a $900,000 purchase, a buyer with no exemption would owe **$16,000 in PTT** on the completion date.

### Additional Example — $1,500,000 Home

| Value Bracket | Amount in Bracket | Rate | Tax Payable |
|---|---|---|---|
| First $200,000 | $200,000 | 1% | $2,000 |
| $200,001 – $1,500,000 | $1,300,000 | 2% | $26,000 |
| **Total PTT** | | | **$28,000** |

## First-Time Home Buyers' Exemption

BC offers a **full or partial PTT exemption** for eligible first-time home buyers. This is one of the most valuable programs available to BC buyers and can eliminate the PTT entirely on qualifying purchases.

### Eligibility Requirements

To qualify for the First-Time Home Buyers' PTT Exemption, **all of the following must apply**:

- You are a **Canadian citizen or permanent resident**
- You have **lived in BC for at least 12 consecutive months** immediately before the date of registration, **or** you have filed **at least two BC income tax returns** in the last six years
- You have **never owned a principal residence anywhere in the world** at any time
- You have **never previously claimed** the first-time home buyer's exemption or refund in BC
- The property will be used as your **principal residence** and you must move in within **92 days** of registration
- You must occupy the home as your principal residence for **at least one full year** after registration

### Exemption Thresholds (Current)

| Purchase Price | Exemption |
|---|---|
| $835,000 or less | **Full exemption** — $0 PTT payable |
| $835,001 – $860,000 | **Partial exemption** — PTT is reduced on a sliding scale |
| Over $860,000 | **No exemption** — full PTT applies |

For properties between $835,001 and $860,000, the exemption is reduced proportionally. Your lawyer or notary will calculate the exact partial exemption amount.

**Potential saving:** On an $800,000 home, the full PTT would be $14,000. With the first-time buyer exemption, this drops to **$0** — a saving of $14,000.

### Part-Interest Purchases

If you are purchasing a property with another person who does not qualify as a first-time buyer, only the **qualifying buyer's proportionate share** of the PTT is exempt. For example, if two buyers each own 50% and only one qualifies, the exemption applies to 50% of the PTT.

## Newly Built Home Exemption

The **Newly Built Home Exemption** reduces or eliminates PTT on purchases of new construction residential properties. This exemption applies to properties that have never been occupied as a place of residence.

### Eligibility Requirements

- The property must be a **newly built home** — including a newly constructed house, a newly built strata unit, a manufactured home, or a home that has been significantly renovated (defined as having had all or substantially all of the building's original structure removed or replaced)
- The purchaser must be an **individual** (not a corporation)
- The property will be used as the buyer's **principal residence**
- The buyer must move in within **92 days** of registration

### Exemption Thresholds (Current)

| Purchase Price | Exemption |
|---|---|
| $1,100,000 or less | **Full exemption** — $0 PTT payable |
| $1,100,001 – $1,150,000 | **Partial exemption** — sliding scale reduction |
| Over $1,150,000 | **No exemption** — full PTT applies |

<Note>
  The Newly Built Home Exemption does **not** require you to be a first-time buyer. Existing homeowners purchasing a newly built principal residence can also claim this exemption, provided all other eligibility criteria are met.
</Note>

## Additional Property Transfer Tax for Foreign Buyers

<Warning>
  **Foreign buyers face an additional 20% PTT** on residential properties located in specified regions of BC. This **Additional Property Transfer Tax (APTT)** applies on top of the standard PTT rates and applies to:

  - **Foreign nationals** (non-Canadian citizens and non-permanent residents)
  - **Foreign corporations** (corporations that are not incorporated in Canada, or are controlled by foreign nationals)
  - **Taxable trustees** holding property in trust for foreign entities

  **Affected Regions:**
  The additional 20% tax currently applies to residential property transfers in the following areas:
  - Metro Vancouver Regional District
  - Fraser Valley Regional District
  - Capital Regional District (Victoria area)
  - Nanaimo Regional District
  - Central Okanagan Regional District

  **Example:** A foreign national purchasing an $800,000 property in Vancouver would owe the standard PTT of $14,000 **plus** an additional $160,000 (20% × $800,000) — for a total PTT liability of **$174,000**.

  Certain exemptions exist (e.g., for protected persons, some work permit holders). Consult a BC real estate lawyer if foreign buyer status may apply to your transaction.
</Warning>

## Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT)

Separate from PTT, BC's **Speculation and Vacancy Tax** is an **annual tax** (not a one-time closing cost) targeting vacant properties and foreign owners in designated urban areas. Unlike PTT, it is assessed each year. Residential homeowners who use their property as a principal residence and are Canadian citizens or permanent residents are generally exempt. If you are purchasing a secondary property or investment property in a designated area, confirm your SVT obligations with a tax advisor.

## How PTT Is Filed at Closing

Your **lawyer or notary** handles the PTT Return on your behalf as part of the closing process. You do not need to file directly with the BC government. The process works as follows:

1. Your legal professional completes the **PTT Return** (and **Schedule A** if claiming an exemption)
2. The PTT amount is included in your **Statement of Adjustments** — funds are collected from you along with the rest of your closing funds
3. The PTT return and payment are submitted to the Land Title and Survey Authority (LTSA) at the time of title registration
4. If claiming an exemption, supporting documentation (residency confirmation, declaration of intended use) must be provided and retained

<Note>
  Retain all documents supporting your exemption claim — including proof of residency, income tax filings, and the purchase contract — for a minimum of **seven years** after closing. The BC Ministry of Finance may audit exemption claims after the fact. If an exemption is found to have been claimed improperly, the full PTT (plus interest and penalties) becomes payable.
</Note>
