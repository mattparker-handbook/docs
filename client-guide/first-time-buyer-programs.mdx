Buying your first home in British Columbia comes with access to a meaningful set of **federal and provincial programs** designed to reduce your upfront costs, lower your tax burden, and make homeownership more accessible. The landscape of first-time buyer programs has evolved significantly in recent years — with the launch of the **First Home Savings Account (FHSA)** in 2023 being the most significant change in a generation. Understanding which programs apply to your situation, and how to combine them effectively, can add tens of thousands of dollars to your down payment capacity.

This guide covers every major program available to BC's first-time buyers as of 2024.

## Detailed Program Descriptions

### 1. First Home Savings Account (FHSA)

The **First Home Savings Account** is a registered account introduced by the federal government on April 1, 2023. It combines the best features of an RRSP and a TFSA specifically for first-time home buyers.

**How it works:**
- Contribute up to **$8,000 per year**, with a **lifetime maximum of $40,000**
- Contributions are **tax-deductible** (like an RRSP) — reducing your taxable income in the year of contribution
- Investment growth inside the account is **tax-sheltered**
- Qualifying withdrawals for a first home purchase are **completely tax-free** (like a TFSA)
- Unused annual contribution room carries forward by up to **$8,000 per year** (one year of carry-forward maximum)
- Accounts can be open for a maximum of **15 years** or until you turn **71**, whichever comes first
- If you don't buy a home, funds can be transferred to an RRSP or RRIF without tax — no contribution room is used

**Eligibility:**
- Canadian resident
- Age 18 or older (19 in provinces with a higher age of majority)
- First-time home buyer: you have not owned a qualifying home that you lived in as a principal residence in the current year or at any time in the preceding **four calendar years**
- You intend to occupy the home as your principal residence within one year of purchase

**Why it matters:** A buyer who opens an FHSA in 2023 and contributes $8,000/year could accumulate **$40,000 in contributions alone** (plus growth) within five years — all tax-free on withdrawal. Combined with the HBP, this is now the most powerful first-time buyer savings tool available.

### 2. RRSP Home Buyers' Plan (HBP)

The **Home Buyers' Plan** has been available since 1992 and allows first-time buyers to make a tax-free withdrawal from their RRSP specifically to purchase or build a qualifying home.

**Key details:**
- Maximum withdrawal: **$35,000 per person** ($70,000 per couple if both qualify)
- Funds must have been in the RRSP for at least **90 days** before withdrawal
- Withdrawals are **not included in your taxable income** in the year of withdrawal
- Repayment begins **two years after the year of withdrawal** and must be completed over a **15-year period**
- Each year, a minimum repayment (1/15th of the total amount) is due — missed repayments are added to your taxable income that year

**Eligibility:**
- First-time home buyer (same definition as FHSA — not having owned a principal residence in the current year or preceding four calendar years)
- Canadian resident at the time of withdrawal
- You must have a written agreement to buy or build a qualifying home
- You must intend to occupy the home as your principal residence within one year of purchase

**Repayment example:** If you withdraw $35,000, your annual minimum repayment is $35,000 ÷ 15 = **$2,333/year** for 15 years. You can repay more in any year to reduce the outstanding balance.

### 3. BC First-Time Home Buyers' PTT Exemption

BC's **Property Transfer Tax (PTT) First-Time Home Buyers' Exemption** eliminates PTT entirely on qualifying purchases up to $835,000 — and provides a partial exemption on purchases between $835,001 and $860,000.

**Benefit:** The full PTT on an $800,000 property would be $14,000. With this exemption, eligible first-time buyers pay **$0**.

**Eligibility:**
- Canadian citizen or permanent resident
- BC resident for at least 12 consecutive months before registration, or have filed two BC income tax returns in the last six years
- Never previously owned a principal residence anywhere in the world
- Never previously claimed this exemption
- Property will be your principal residence
- You must move in within **92 days** of registration
- You must occupy the home as your principal residence for at least **one full year** after registration

**Thresholds:**

| Purchase Price | PTT Payable |
|---|---|
| $835,000 or less | $0 (full exemption) |
| $835,001 – $860,000 | Partial exemption (sliding scale) |
| $860,001 and above | Full PTT applies |

See our full [Property Transfer Tax guide](/client-guide/property-transfer-tax) for rate tables, calculations, and additional exemption details.

### 4. GST/HST New Housing Rebate

When purchasing a **newly constructed home** in BC, 5% GST applies to the purchase price. The **GST/HST New Housing Rebate** provides a partial refund of the GST paid, for homes purchased as a primary residence.

**Rebate structure:**
- Available on new homes with a purchase price below **$450,000**
- Maximum rebate: **$6,300** (for homes priced at $350,000 or less)
- The rebate phases out gradually between $350,000 and $450,000
- No rebate is available for new homes priced at $450,000 or more

**How it works in BC:** Builders typically factor the rebate into the purchase price and apply for it on your behalf — confirm with your builder whether the listed price is GST-inclusive or exclusive.

**Owner-built homes:** If you are building your own home, you apply for the rebate directly after construction is complete.

<Note>
  In BC, there is no PST on new home purchases (unlike Ontario where HST applies). However, GST still applies to all new construction. Always confirm the GST treatment with your realtor and builder before finalizing your purchase agreement, as the listed price may or may not include GST.
</Note>

### 5. First-Time Home Buyer Incentive (FTHBI)

<Note>
  **This program has ended.** The federal First-Time Home Buyer Incentive accepted its last applications on **March 21, 2024**, and the program has since been discontinued. No new applications are being accepted. If you previously received an Incentive, your existing agreement with the Government of Canada remains in effect.

  The **First Home Savings Account (FHSA)** is the federal government's primary tool for supporting first-time buyers going forward. If you were considering the FTHBI, speak with your mortgage broker about how the FHSA can serve a similar goal of increasing your down payment capacity.
</Note>

The FTHBI was a shared-equity mortgage program where the Government of Canada contributed 5% or 10% of the purchase price as an interest-free loan in exchange for a proportionate share in the property's future value. Its discontinuation reflects a federal policy shift toward the FHSA as the preferred first-time buyer support mechanism.

### 6. BC Home Owner Grant

The **BC Home Owner Grant** is an **annual grant** that reduces the amount of property tax you pay on your principal residence. While it is not exclusive to first-time buyers, it applies from your very first year of ownership and provides ongoing savings throughout your homeownership.

**Basic grant amounts (2024):**
- **$570** for properties in the Capital Regional District, Metro Vancouver, and the Fraser Valley Regional District
- **$770** for properties in all other areas of BC

**Additional grants** are available for seniors (65+), veterans, and persons with disabilities — bringing the total grant to as high as $1,045 in some regions.

**Eligibility:**
- The property must be your **principal residence** in BC
- You must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident
- You must have been a BC resident on December 31 of the previous year
- The property's **assessed value** must fall within program limits:
  - Basic grant: available on properties assessed up to **$2,125,000** (2024)
  - The grant is reduced for properties assessed between $2,025,000 and $2,125,000

**How to claim:** You must apply for the Home Owner Grant each year by the property tax due date (usually July 2 in most municipalities). Applications are made through the BC government's online portal or through your municipality.

## Stacking FHSA and HBP: Maximize Your Down Payment

<Tip>
  **Use both the FHSA and the HBP together for the maximum down payment boost.** A couple who each open FHSAs and contribute the maximum for five years, while also each building up $35,000 in RRSP savings for the HBP, could access:

  | Source | Per Person | Combined (Couple) |
  |---|---|---|
  | FHSA (max) | $40,000 | $80,000 |
  | RRSP HBP (max) | $35,000 | $70,000 |
  | **Total** | **$75,000** | **$150,000** |

  This is in addition to any regular savings. On a $900,000 home requiring a minimum $50,000 down payment (with default insurance), a combined pool of $150,000 would not only clear the minimum — it could bring the down payment to 20%, **eliminating the need for mortgage default insurance entirely** and saving an additional $23,400+ in insurance premiums.

  Talk to a mortgage broker about structuring your savings plan across both accounts well before you plan to purchase.
</Tip>

## Getting Started

The best time to open an FHSA and begin contributing is **as early as possible** — even if you're not yet planning to buy. Contribution room accumulates whether or not you make a purchase, and the tax deductions begin immediately. If you're already planning to buy within the next 1–2 years, confirm your eligibility for the PTT Exemption and the HBP with your mortgage broker before your offer is submitted.
