British Columbia is one of Canada's primary destinations for newcomers — immigrants, permanent residents, temporary foreign workers, and international students who establish roots and begin building their financial lives here. Many of your clients in this demographic are ready and motivated homebuyers who face a specific challenge: a thin or absent Canadian credit history that doesn't reflect their actual financial strength. The good news is that major Canadian lenders have developed programs specifically designed to bridge that gap. This guide gives Realtors and Financial Planners a working framework for identifying and referring newcomer clients effectively.

## Eligibility Categories

Not all newcomers are treated the same by mortgage lenders. The eligibility framework depends primarily on immigration status, length of time in Canada, and whether the borrower can demonstrate credit responsibility through alternative documentation.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Permanent Residents" icon="id-card">
    Permanent residents qualify for mortgages under the same framework as Canadian citizens at virtually all lenders. A PR card or Confirmation of Permanent Residence (COPR) is sufficient proof of status. The qualification challenge is typically limited to **credit history** — a new PR who arrived recently may not yet have a Canadian credit bureau file, even though their immigration status is fully established.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Temporary Residents (Work Permit)" icon="briefcase">
    Temporary resident workers are eligible for mortgage financing at **select lenders** under specific programs. Requirements are more stringent: minimum 35% down payment is common (though some programs allow less for PRs-in-progress), the work permit must have a minimum remaining validity, and the borrower must be employed in Canada at the time of application. Not all work permit types are treated equally — open work permits are generally viewed more favourably than employer-specific permits.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Newcomers Without Canadian Credit History" icon="star">
    The most common challenge for newcomers — regardless of immigration status — is the absence of a Canadian credit bureau profile. This is addressed through **specialized new-to-Canada programs** that accept alternative credit documentation in lieu of a traditional bureau score. These programs are offered by the major chartered banks and insured lenders under CMHC's New to Canada program guidelines.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## New to Canada Mortgage Programs

The following programs are the most commonly used for newcomer mortgage qualification. Program availability, terms, and criteria are subject to change; confirm current details at time of referral.

| Program | Lender | Minimum Down Payment | Credit History Required | Key Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CMHC New to Canada | All CMHC-approved insured lenders | 5% (under $500K) / 10% ($500K–$999K) | International credit bureau report **or** 12 months of alternative credit history in Canada | Both PRs and eligible temporary residents; income verified through employment letter |
| RBC Newcomer Advantage | Royal Bank of Canada | 5–35% (status dependent) | Work permit eligible; alternative credit accepted | Broad eligibility for recent PRs and TWP holders; strong bank relationship often accelerates approval |
| Scotiabank StartRight Mortgage | Scotiabank | 5% for PRs | PR or visa holder; alternative credit documentation accepted | Strong program for recent arrivals; part of Scotiabank's broader StartRight newcomer banking suite |
| TD New to Canada | TD Bank | 5% for PRs | Permanent resident with minimum 3 months of Canadian credit history | Requires established TD banking relationship in many cases; competitive rates |
| BMO Newcomer Mortgage | BMO | 5% | PR; alternative credit accepted | Less publicized program; worth considering for clients with BMO banking history |

## Alternative Credit Documentation Accepted

When a borrower has no Canadian credit bureau file or a file that is too thin for traditional qualification, lenders with newcomer programs will accept the following as evidence of credit responsibility:

<Steps>
  <Step title="International Credit Bureau Report">
    A formal credit report from the borrower's home country — obtained through a service such as Equifax's international credit reporting or Nova Credit (which provides auto-translated credit reports from select countries) — is the most direct substitute for a Canadian bureau file. Accepted countries and bureaus vary by lender.
  </Step>
  <Step title="12 Months of On-Time Rental Payment History">
    A letter from a former landlord or property manager (on letterhead where possible) confirming 12 consecutive months of on-time rent payments, supported by bank statements showing the corresponding payments, is widely accepted by newcomer program lenders.
  </Step>
  <Step title="12 Months of Bank Statements Showing Stability">
    Bank statements from both the borrower's home country and their Canadian account showing consistent savings, responsible cash flow management, and no returned payments or overdraft history. This supports the overall credit picture in the absence of a bureau file.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Utility and Telecom Payment History">
    Evidence of 12 consecutive months of on-time payment for utilities (hydro, gas, internet) or mobile phone contracts in Canada. These are typically not captured by the credit bureau but demonstrate credit behaviour. Supported by statements and bank records.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Employment Letter and Income Verification">
    A current employment letter confirming position, tenure, annual salary, and permanent or continuing employment status. For newcomers, a strong employment profile — particularly with a recognized Canadian employer — carries significant weight in the overall credit assessment.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Down Payment Requirements and Sources

Down payment requirements for newcomers vary by program, but sourcing and documentation are subject to the same rules as all other applicants:

- **Minimum 5%** for insured programs (under $1M purchase price) for permanent residents who meet program credit requirements
- **Minimum 35%** for temporary resident / work permit holders at most lenders (some programs allow less under specific conditions)
- Down payment must be verified with **90 days of bank statements** showing the funds were accumulated (not borrowed)
- **Gifted down payments** from immediate family members are accepted with a signed gift letter; the relationship between donor and recipient must be confirmed
- Funds held in a home country bank account are acceptable — the source must be documented and the funds must have been in the account for at least 90 days

<Tip>
  The single most impactful action a newcomer client can take — ideally on or shortly after arrival — is to **establish Canadian credit immediately**. A secured credit card (where the borrower deposits funds as collateral for the credit limit) is available to individuals with no Canadian credit history and begins building a bureau file from day one. Many major banks offer credit-builder products specifically for newcomers. Six to twelve months of on-time payment on even a modest secured card can meaningfully improve lender options and rate access by the time the client is ready to purchase.
</Tip>

<Note>
  **Foreign Buyer Ban — What Realtors and Financial Planners Should Know**

  The federal **Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act** restricts non-Canadians from purchasing residential property in Canada in designated areas. The ban applies to **non-Canadian individuals and foreign commercial enterprises**. Key points for client referrals:

  - **Permanent residents are exempt** — the ban does not apply to PRs, who are treated the same as Canadian citizens for real estate purchases.
  - **Work permit holders may be exempt** — temporary residents who have held a valid work permit for at least 183 days in the preceding year and have not purchased under the exemption previously may qualify for an exemption. Specific conditions apply.
  - **Residential property is broadly defined** — the ban covers single-family homes, semi-detached, condos, and similar residential properties in census metropolitan areas and census agglomerations.
  - **Penalties apply** — non-compliant purchases can result in fines and a court-ordered sale of the property.

  Clients whose residency status may be affected by the foreign buyer ban should receive legal advice from a qualified BC real estate lawyer before any offer is written. We can refer clients to appropriate legal professionals as part of the intake process.
</Note>
