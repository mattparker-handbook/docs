GDS and TDS are the two core ratios lenders use to determine whether a borrower can service a mortgage. Understanding how they work — and what moves them — lets you have smarter pre-referral conversations with clients and set accurate expectations before a deal is on the table. Every lender runs these calculations; the thresholds vary slightly by lender type and insurance status, but the underlying formulas are universal.

## Gross Debt Service (GDS) Ratio

The GDS ratio measures the share of a borrower's gross monthly income consumed by the housing costs on the property being purchased. It captures the cost of ownership itself, independent of any other debts the borrower carries.

**Formula:**

```
GDS = (P + I + T + H + 50% Condo Fees) ÷ Gross Monthly Income × 100
```

**Where PITH equals:**
- **P** — Principal repayment (monthly mortgage payment, principal component)
- **I** — Interest (monthly mortgage payment, interest component)
- **T** — Property taxes (monthly equivalent; annual taxes ÷ 12)
- **H** — Heat (lenders typically use $150/month as a standard estimate if actual costs are unknown)
- **+ 50% of monthly condo fees** (if the property is a strata unit)

**Maximum Thresholds:**
- **39%** for insured mortgages (purchases under $1.5M with less than 20% down, effective December 15, 2024)
- Up to **44%** for strong conventional files at select lenders

**Example Calculation:**

A client is purchasing a condo in Vancouver for $850,000 with 10% down ($85,000). Monthly mortgage payment: $4,200. Property taxes: $300/month. Heat: $150/month. Condo fees: $600/month (50% = $300). Gross monthly income: $14,000.

```
GDS = ($4,200 + $300 + $150 + $300) ÷ $14,000 × 100
GDS = $4,950 ÷ $14,000 × 100
GDS = 35.4%  ✅ Below the 39% insured threshold
```

## Total Debt Service (TDS) Ratio

The TDS ratio adds all other recurring debt obligations to the GDS calculation. It measures total debt load relative to gross income — and is often the binding constraint for clients who carry car loans, student debt, or significant credit card balances.

**Formula:**

```
TDS = (PITH + 50% Condo Fees + All Other Monthly Debt Payments) ÷ Gross Monthly Income × 100
```

**"All Other Debt Payments" includes:**
- Car loan and lease payments
- Student loan minimum payments
- Credit card minimum payments (lenders use **3% of the outstanding balance**, regardless of actual minimum)
- Lines of credit minimum payments
- Other mortgage payments (investment properties, co-signed obligations)
- Spousal/child support payments (where legally required)

**Maximum Thresholds:**
- **44%** for insured mortgages
- Up to **50%** for conventional files at select lenders with strong qualifying profiles

**Example Calculation (continuing from above):**

Same client with a $550/month car loan, a $200/month student loan payment, and $8,000 in credit card balances (3% = $240/month):

```
TDS = ($4,950 + $550 + $200 + $240) ÷ $14,000 × 100
TDS = $5,940 ÷ $14,000 × 100
TDS = 42.4%  ✅ Below the 44% insured threshold — but close
```

## GDS/TDS Quick Reference

| Ratio | What It Includes | Max — Insured Mortgage | Max — Conventional Mortgage |
|---|---|---|---|
| GDS | Principal + Interest + Property Taxes + Heat + 50% of Condo Fees | 39% | 39–44% (lender dependent) |
| TDS | Everything in GDS + all other monthly debt payments | 44% | 44–50% (lender dependent) |

## Common Debt Payments That Impact TDS

The following are the most common items that inflate TDS and reduce a client's qualifying purchase price. Financial Planners in particular should review these with clients before a referral:

- **Credit cards** — Lenders use **3% of the outstanding balance** as the imputed monthly payment, not the actual statement minimum. A client with $20,000 in combined credit card balances carries a $600/month TDS impact even if they pay the minimum of $400.
- **Car loans and leases** — Full contractual monthly payment is used. A $650/month car payment on a $60,000 vehicle purchase is a significant TDS drag.
- **Lines of credit** — The minimum payment (typically 1–3% of the balance or a fixed minimum, depending on the facility) is included. A $50,000 HELOC with a $500/month minimum counts in full.
- **Student loans** — Minimum monthly payment included, even if deferred. Some lenders include deferred student loan payments using an imputed repayment schedule.
- **Other mortgages** — Co-signed mortgages and investment property mortgages are included unless the rental income sufficiently offsets them (see the Rental Portfolio Structuring guide).

<Tip>
  If a client's TDS is close to the threshold, paying down a revolving credit line (credit card or line of credit) before submitting an application can have an outsized effect on their qualifying ratio. A $10,000 credit card paydown reduces the TDS-imputed monthly debt payment by $300 — which, depending on income, can increase the maximum qualifying mortgage by $50,000 or more. Encourage clients to do this 30–60 days before application so the updated balance reports to the credit bureau before the lender pulls their file.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  Lenders do **not** use the actual minimum payment shown on a credit card statement. They use **3% of the outstanding balance** as the imputed monthly payment, regardless of what the card issuer requires. A client who pays their $15,000 balance in full each month but carries an average month-end balance of $15,000 will have $450 added to their TDS calculation. Clients who routinely carry high balances — even if they're responsible payers — may be surprised by how much their credit card habits affect their mortgage qualification.
</Warning>
