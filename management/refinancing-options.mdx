Refinancing your mortgage means replacing your existing mortgage with a new one — typically with different terms, a different lender, or a larger balance that allows you to access built-up home equity. Unlike renewal (which happens automatically at the end of your term), refinancing is a deliberate financial decision that can happen at any point during your mortgage term. Done at the right time and for the right reasons, refinancing can save you significant money or unlock capital for other goals. Done carelessly, it can cost more than it saves.

## Common Reasons to Refinance

Homeowners in BC refinance for a range of financial goals:

- **Access home equity** — fund a renovation, make an investment, or consolidate high-interest debt by pulling equity out of your home
- **Secure a lower interest rate** — if rates have dropped significantly since you took out your mortgage, refinancing can reduce your monthly payment and total interest cost
- **Change your amortization** — extend to reduce monthly payments or shorten to pay off faster
- **Adjust your mortgage term** — switch from a longer to a shorter term to align with rate expectations
- **Add or remove a borrower** — life changes such as marriage, separation, or the addition of a co-signer sometimes require restructuring the mortgage
- **Switch from variable to fixed (or vice versa)** — change your rate type to better match your financial risk tolerance or current market conditions

## How Much Can You Refinance For?

Under OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions) regulations, the maximum you can refinance your mortgage to is **80% of your home's appraised value** (loan-to-value ratio, or LTV). This applies to all federally regulated lenders in Canada, including the major banks.

**Example:**

- Home value: $700,000
- Maximum refinance (80% LTV): $560,000
- Current outstanding mortgage: $400,000
- **Maximum new equity accessible: $160,000**

Keep in mind that your home will be appraised at the time of refinancing, and the value used is the lender's appraised value — not necessarily your estimate or a recent purchase price. If your home has appreciated significantly, you may have more accessible equity than you expect.

<Note>
  If you need to access more than 80% LTV, a second mortgage through a private lender may be an option, though at significantly higher interest rates. See the [Equity Takeout guide](/management/equity-takeout) for more detail.
</Note>

## Refinance Costs

Refinancing is not free. Before proceeding, you need to understand the full cost picture so you can determine whether the financial benefit justifies the expense.

| Cost Item | Typical Range |
|---|---|
| Prepayment penalty | $0–$25,000+ (depends on existing mortgage) |
| Legal / notary fees | $1,000–$2,000 |
| Appraisal | $300–$600 |
| Title insurance | $200–$400 |
| Lender fee (if applicable) | $0–$1,000 |

The prepayment penalty is almost always the largest cost and the one that varies the most. Fixed-rate mortgages typically carry an Interest Rate Differential (IRD) penalty, which can be very large when market rates are lower than your contract rate. Variable-rate mortgages typically carry a three-month interest penalty, which is much more predictable.

<Warning>
  **Refinancing mid-term can be costly.** The prepayment penalty on a fixed-rate mortgage can easily reach $10,000–$25,000 or more, depending on your rate, remaining term, and lender. This penalty can completely wipe out any interest savings from a lower rate. Always get your exact penalty figure in writing from your current lender before proceeding. Run the numbers carefully before signing anything.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  If your mortgage maturity date is within 12 months, it's often worth waiting to refinance at renewal rather than breaking early. You'll avoid the prepayment penalty entirely, and many lenders will allow a blend-and-extend or early renewal within 120 days of maturity. Ask your broker to model both scenarios.
</Tip>

## The Refinance Process

<Steps>
  <Step title="Calculate Your Equity and Define Your Goal">
    Determine your home's approximate current value (a licensed appraiser or your broker can help estimate this), your outstanding mortgage balance, and how much equity you want to access. Be clear on what the funds will be used for — this helps structure the refinance correctly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get a Penalty Quote from Your Current Lender">
    Contact your current lender and request a formal prepayment penalty calculation. Ask for it in writing. This number is essential before you can evaluate whether refinancing makes financial sense right now or whether waiting until renewal is smarter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run a Break-Even Analysis">
    Add up all refinancing costs (penalty + legal + appraisal + other fees). Compare that total against the monthly savings or financial benefit you'll gain from the new mortgage. Divide total costs by monthly savings to find your break-even point in months. If you expect to keep the property beyond that break-even point, refinancing may make sense.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply for the Refinance">
    Work with your mortgage broker to find the best available product for your goal. Submit your application with full income verification, property details, and documentation of the intended use of funds (some lenders require this for debt consolidation or investment purposes).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close with a Notary or Lawyer">
    In BC, a notary or lawyer must complete the refinance. They will discharge your existing mortgage, register the new mortgage against title, and advance the funds. Allow 2–4 weeks for the process once your commitment letter is issued.
  </Step>
</Steps>
