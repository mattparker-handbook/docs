Your mortgage renewal is one of the most important financial decisions you'll make as a homeowner. Unlike your original purchase, renewal doesn't come with the same sense of urgency or excitement — which means it's easy to simply sign whatever your lender sends and move on. But the choices you make at renewal directly affect the interest rate you'll pay, the flexibility you'll have, and how quickly you'll build equity over your next term. Taking the time to shop around and plan ahead can save you tens of thousands of dollars.

## When to Start Your Renewal Process

The ideal time to begin your renewal planning is **120 days (four months) before your maturity date**. Most lenders will send you a renewal offer around this time, and many will honour an early rate hold so you can lock in a competitive rate before conditions change.

Starting early gives you time to:
- Compare offers from multiple lenders and mortgage brokers
- Confirm your maturity date and request a penalty quote if you're considering moving before your term ends
- Complete any required documentation without the pressure of a looming deadline
- Negotiate better terms rather than accepting the first offer

<Tip>
  Mark your maturity date in your calendar the moment you sign your mortgage. Set a reminder for 120 days prior so renewal planning doesn't sneak up on you.
</Tip>

## Renewal, Switch or Refinance: What's the Difference?

You have three options at renewal. **Switching lenders does not automatically mean refinancing.**

### Renew with your current lender

You agree to a new rate and term with your existing lender, without increasing the mortgage balance or restructuring the loan. The existing mortgage charge stays registered on title, and a straightforward renewal generally does not require new legal registration work.

### Switch lenders at renewal

A **straight switch** moves your existing mortgage balance to a new lender, generally keeping the remaining amortization and without taking out additional money. For an eligible transfer, the existing mortgage charge stays on title and is transferred or assigned to the new lender, rather than discharged and replaced with a new registration.

**A switch at renewal often has no fees payable by you.** Many receiving lenders cover eligible transfer or assignment costs, and sometimes appraisal costs, as part of their switch program. Legal or transfer processing may still happen behind the scenes, but that does not automatically mean you receive a legal bill.

We'll confirm whether your mortgage qualifies, what the new lender covers, and whether any administration, discharge, appraisal or other charges would be payable by you before you proceed. Some collateral charges, linked credit facilities or lender requirements may mean a discharge and new registration are needed instead of a straight transfer.

### Refinance at renewal

A **refinance** changes the borrowing — for example, increasing the mortgage to access equity or consolidate debt, or extending the amortization beyond what a straight switch permits. You may refinance with your current lender or a different lender.

Unlike an eligible straight switch, refinancing involves **a new mortgage charge registration on title and legal fees**. Appraisal and other lender costs may also apply. We'll review those costs alongside the payment, interest and long-term impact before recommending a refinance.

<Tip>
  If you only want a better rate or different lender terms and don't need additional borrowing, ask about a straight switch first. It may give you the benefits of changing lenders without out-of-pocket transfer fees.
</Tip>

## The Renewal Process Step by Step

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review Your Current Mortgage Details">
    Pull out your current mortgage documents and confirm your maturity date, outstanding balance, current rate, amortization remaining, and any prepayment privileges you've used. This is your baseline for evaluating whether to stay, switch or refinance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Engage a Mortgage Broker (120+ Days Out)">
    Contact your mortgage broker well before renewal. A broker can access rates from dozens of lenders simultaneously and identify whether your current lender's offer is competitive. This step costs you nothing and gives you significant negotiating leverage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate Your Lender's Renewal Offer">
    When your lender sends a renewal offer, don't sign it immediately. Review the rate, term options, prepayment privileges, and any restrictions. Compare it directly against what your broker has sourced from competing lenders.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare a Straight Switch with a Refinance">
    If another lender offers a better rate or more suitable terms, check whether you qualify for a straight switch with covered transfer costs. If you also want to borrow more or restructure the mortgage, compare a refinance separately, including its legal and registration costs. We'll confirm any fees you would pay rather than assuming every lender change requires a legal bill.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit Your Renewal, Switch or Refinance Application">
    Once you've made a decision, complete the required paperwork. Renewing with your current lender is usually straightforward. Switching or refinancing requires lender approval, income verification and sometimes an appraisal — start early so everything can be completed by your maturity date.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign Your Mortgage Documents">
    Review your mortgage commitment carefully before signing. Confirm the rate, term, payment amount, prepayment privileges, conditions and any fees payable by you. For an eligible straight switch, the transfer provider handles the assignment of the existing charge. For a refinance or a switch that cannot be transferred, a lawyer, notary or lender-appointed provider handles the discharge and new registration.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Should You Stay, Switch or Refinance?

The right choice depends on your goals, the mortgage terms and the total cost — not just the rate.

| Factor | Renew with Current Lender | Straight Switch at Renewal | Refinance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lender | Same lender | New lender | Same or new lender |
| Borrowing | Existing balance; no new money | Existing balance; no new money | May increase borrowing or change structure |
| Title charge | Existing charge stays in place | Existing charge transferred or assigned, where eligible | New mortgage charge registration |
| Client fees | Usually none for a straightforward renewal | Often none when the receiving lender covers eligible costs; confirm any exceptions | Legal and registration fees; appraisal or other costs may apply |
| Paperwork | Usually limited | New lender application and supporting documents | New approval and supporting documents |
| Appraisal | Usually not required | May be required; coverage varies | May be required |

Staying can make sense when your lender offers competitive terms and the mortgage still suits your needs. A straight switch is worth considering when another lender offers better overall value — especially when eligible transfer costs are covered. Refinancing is a separate decision when you need equity, debt consolidation or a change in mortgage structure; weigh the legal costs and long-term borrowing impact alongside any payment savings.

<Note>
  Moving the mortgage **on its maturity date** generally avoids an early-break prepayment penalty. Moving before that date may trigger a penalty, even if the receiving lender covers transfer costs. We'll confirm the timing and your existing lender's payout requirements.
</Note>

<Tip>
  Never sign your lender's first renewal offer without shopping it around first. Lenders routinely send renewal letters at posted rates — well above what's available to a qualified borrower who takes the time to ask. A five-minute conversation with a broker can easily save you thousands of dollars.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  **Automatic renewal risk:** If you do nothing before your maturity date, most lenders will automatically renew your mortgage — typically at a posted or "default" rate, which is among the highest rates available. This can cost you significantly more than a negotiated or brokered renewal. Always respond to your renewal offer before the deadline.
</Warning>

## What to Negotiate at Renewal

Even if you plan to stay with your current lender, many terms can be reviewed at renewal. Consider asking about:

- **Rate reduction** — use competing offers as leverage
- **Amortization extension** — can lower your payment, but may require a refinance and increase total interest; it is not automatically part of a straight renewal or switch
- **Prepayment privileges** — ensure you have the flexibility you need (10–20% lump sum, payment increase)
- **Portability** — important if you may move before your next renewal
- **Shorter term** — if you expect rates to drop, a 1–2 year term may outperform a 5-year lock-in

The renewal conversation is also a good time to reassess your overall financial picture. If your income has increased, your home has appreciated, or your goals have changed, renewal is the moment to recalibrate your mortgage structure accordingly.
