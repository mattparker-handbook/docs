Prepayment privileges are one of the most underused features of a Canadian mortgage. They allow you to pay down your principal faster than your regular payment schedule requires — without triggering a prepayment penalty. Over a 25-year amortization, even modest use of these features can shave years off your mortgage and save tens of thousands of dollars in interest. Yet most borrowers never use them, either because they don't know they exist or because they assume they're too complicated.

## Types of Prepayment Privileges

Most Canadian lenders offer three types of prepayment privileges as part of a standard mortgage:

**Lump Sum Payments**
You can make one or more lump sum payments against your mortgage principal, typically up to 10–20% of the original mortgage balance per year, penalty-free. The payment reduces your principal directly, which means every future payment has a slightly lower interest component. The annual window usually resets on your anniversary date.

**Payment Increase**
You can increase your regular scheduled payment by a set percentage (typically 10–20%) per year. The increase goes directly toward principal, accelerating your payoff without changing your payment frequency or renegotiating your mortgage.

**Double-Up Payments**
Some lenders allow you to double your regular scheduled payment at any time — essentially making two payments in one. Like a lump sum, the extra amount is applied directly to principal. Not all lenders offer this option; check your mortgage documents or call your lender to confirm.

## The Real Impact of Prepayments

The numbers below illustrate what prepayment strategies can mean on a typical BC mortgage. Small, consistent actions compound significantly over time.

| Scenario | $500K at 5.5% / 25yr Amortization | Interest Saved vs. Baseline |
|---|---|---|
| No prepayments | ~$431,000 total interest | — |
| $10,000 lump sum in Year 1 | ~$420,000 total interest | ~$11,000 |
| Accelerated bi-weekly payments | ~$391,000 total interest | ~$40,000 |
| Both strategies combined | ~$375,000 total interest | ~$56,000 |

These figures are approximations and vary with rate changes at renewal — but they illustrate the principle clearly. Getting to a paid-off home faster is not just about discipline; it's about using the tools your lender has already built into your mortgage.

<Tip>
  **Time your lump sum payments to your anniversary date.** Many lenders require lump sum prepayments to be made on or around your mortgage anniversary date to qualify as penalty-free. Some allow them at any time throughout the year. Check your specific terms before making a payment — if you miss the window, you may face a penalty, or the payment may be applied differently than you expect.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  **Restricted (no-frills) mortgages may not allow prepayments.** Some lenders offer discounted "no-frills" or "low-rate" mortgage products that trade prepayment privileges for a lower interest rate. If you're on one of these products, you may not be able to make lump sum payments or increase your payment at all during the term. Before making any extra payments, confirm with your lender that they are permitted under your specific product. Applying funds to a restricted mortgage without authorization can trigger an unexpected penalty.
</Warning>

## How to Make a Prepayment

The process varies by lender, but typically you can:

1. **Call your lender** to confirm your available prepayment room and anniversary date
2. **Log into your online banking portal** — many lenders allow lump sum payments directly through their app or website
3. **Set up a payment increase** through your lender's online system or by speaking with a mortgage specialist
4. **Keep records** of every prepayment you make, including the date and amount applied

If you receive a bonus, tax refund, inheritance, or other windfall, consider directing all or part of it toward your mortgage principal before spending it elsewhere. The interest saved is a guaranteed, tax-free return.
