Payment frequency is one of the simplest levers you can pull on your mortgage — and it costs you nothing to change. By adjusting how often you make payments, you can meaningfully reduce the total interest you pay over the life of your loan and shorten your amortization by years. Most borrowers default to monthly payments because that's what they were set up with, but the accelerated options available at every major Canadian lender can make a significant difference without requiring any increase to your overall budget.

## Payment Frequency Options

Canadian lenders offer several payment frequency options. Each has a different payment amount and total number of payments per year.

| Frequency | Payments per Year | How It's Calculated |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | 12 | Standard mortgage payment |
| Semi-monthly | 24 | Monthly payment ÷ 2 |
| Bi-weekly (regular) | 26 | Monthly × 12 ÷ 26 |
| Bi-weekly (accelerated) | 26 | Monthly payment ÷ 2 |
| Weekly (regular) | 52 | Monthly × 12 ÷ 52 |
| Weekly (accelerated) | 52 | Monthly payment ÷ 4 |

The critical distinction between regular and accelerated frequencies is in how the payment is calculated — and therefore how much you're actually paying per year.

## Accelerated vs. Regular Bi-Weekly

The difference between regular bi-weekly and accelerated bi-weekly is subtle but powerful.

- **Regular bi-weekly:** Your monthly payment is divided by 26 payment periods. You're paying the same annual total as monthly — just spread across more payments.
- **Accelerated bi-weekly:** Your monthly payment is divided by 2 and then paid 26 times per year. Because you're paying half your monthly payment 26 times instead of 24 times, you effectively make **one extra full monthly payment per year**.

That one extra payment per year goes entirely toward principal reduction. Over a 25-year amortization, the compounding effect is substantial.

**Example: $500,000 mortgage at 5.5% over 25 years**

| Payment Strategy | Approximate Amortization | Approximate Total Interest |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | 25 years | ~$431,000 |
| Regular bi-weekly | 25 years | ~$428,000 |
| Accelerated bi-weekly | ~22 years | ~$391,000 |

Switching from monthly to accelerated bi-weekly on a $500,000 mortgage saves approximately **3 years of payments** and **$40,000 in interest** — at no additional monthly cost to your budget.

<Tip>
  **Switching to accelerated bi-weekly payments is one of the easiest mortgage optimizations available to you.** Most lenders allow this change at no cost, and it takes a single phone call or online form to set up. If you are currently on monthly or regular bi-weekly payments, contact your lender today and ask to switch. There is almost never a reason not to.
</Tip>

<Note>
  **Changing payment frequency is typically permitted once per year** at most lenders, at no charge. If you are within your term and want to change frequency, call your lender or log into your online banking portal to check what's available and when the change will take effect. Some lenders require advance notice before the next payment date.
</Note>

## Does Your Pay Schedule Matter?

Many borrowers find it easier to manage cash flow by aligning their mortgage payment to their pay cycle. If you're paid bi-weekly, switching to accelerated bi-weekly payments means your mortgage comes out right after each paycheck — making budgeting more intuitive. If you're paid semi-monthly (twice a month on fixed dates), semi-monthly mortgage payments may align better.

The payment frequency you choose should reflect both the financial benefit and your practical cash flow management. The accelerated bi-weekly option remains the best choice for most salaried borrowers who are paid on a bi-weekly schedule.

## Changing Your Frequency During a Term

If you want to switch payment frequencies mid-term:

1. **Contact your lender** — most allow one free change per calendar year
2. **Confirm the effective date** — changes typically take effect at the next payment cycle
3. **Review your amortization** — your lender can provide an updated amortization schedule showing your new projected payoff date
4. **No re-qualification required** — this is an administrative change, not a mortgage restructure

Payment frequency changes do not affect your interest rate, outstanding balance, or mortgage term. They simply change how often you make payments and, in the case of accelerated options, how much you pay in total each year.
