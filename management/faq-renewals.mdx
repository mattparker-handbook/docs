sidebarTitle: "Renewal FAQ"
description: "Answers to the most common questions about renewing your Canadian mortgage, including timing, switching lenders, rate negotiation, and what happens if you miss your renewal date."
---

Mortgage renewal is something every Canadian homeowner encounters, yet the process raises a lot of questions — especially when your financial goals or market conditions have changed since you first took out your mortgage. The answers below address the questions clients ask most often at renewal time.

<Accordion title="When should I start thinking about renewing my mortgage?">
  Start your renewal process at least **120 days (four months) before your maturity date**. Most lenders will send you a renewal offer around the 120-day mark, and many will hold a rate for you early to protect against rising rates. Beginning early gives you time to compare offers from multiple lenders, work with a broker, and complete any required paperwork without the pressure of a deadline. Waiting until your renewal notice arrives — which can be as little as 30 days before maturity — leaves very little room to negotiate or switch.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I switch lenders at renewal without paying a penalty?">
  Yes — switching lenders at maturity (your renewal date) does not trigger a prepayment penalty. The penalty only applies when you break your mortgage before the maturity date. Switching at renewal is the ideal time to shop around because you're free to take the best offer available without any cost penalty. You will, however, need to pay legal fees to discharge your old mortgage and register the new one — typically $1,000–$1,500. The rate savings from switching often far exceed that cost.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is my lender's renewal offer negotiable?">
  Absolutely. Lenders routinely send renewal offers at posted rates — often well above the rates available to a qualified borrower who asks. You have every right to counter your lender's offer with a competing rate you've received from another lender or broker. In many cases, lenders will match or beat competing offers to retain your business. Always get at least one external rate comparison before accepting your lender's offer, even if you intend to stay.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens if I miss my renewal date?">
  If you don't respond to your renewal offer by your maturity date, most lenders will automatically renew your mortgage — typically into a short-term product (often a 6-month closed term) at a **posted rate**, which is one of the highest rates your lender offers. This automatic renewal is designed to protect the lender, not you. You can then renew into a better product once that short term ends, but you will have paid a higher rate in the interim. Always respond to your renewal offer before the deadline, even if just to request more time.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I change my amortization at renewal?">
  Yes. Renewal is one of the best times to adjust your amortization. If you want to reduce your payment, you can extend your amortization (subject to your lender's maximum — typically 25 or 30 years depending on the product and your remaining balance). If you want to pay off your mortgage faster, you can shorten the amortization, which will increase your payment but reduce your total interest. Extending the amortization at renewal does not require a new appraisal if you're staying with your current lender, but switching lenders with an amortization extension may require one.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I add a co-borrower at renewal?">
  Yes, you can add a co-borrower at renewal, but doing so requires a full re-qualification of the mortgage with the new borrower included. Both borrowers' income, credit, and debt ratios will be assessed by the lender. If you're switching lenders to add a co-borrower, legal documentation and a new mortgage registration are required. This is a useful option if your income situation has changed or you want to add a partner or family member to the title. Consult a broker to understand how the co-borrower's financial profile affects the overall application.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do I need to re-qualify to renew with the same lender?">
  In most cases, no. If you are renewing with your current lender and not making any material changes to the mortgage (amount, amortization, borrowers), most lenders will offer a straight renewal without requiring full re-qualification or a stress test. This is one of the advantages of staying with your existing lender, particularly if your income or credit situation has changed since you first qualified. However, if you are switching lenders, increasing your mortgage balance, or significantly extending your amortization, a new qualification and stress test will apply.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What is a blend-and-extend mortgage?">
  A blend-and-extend is a product some lenders offer that allows you to lock in a new rate before your maturity date without paying a prepayment penalty. Your existing rate and the current market rate are "blended" to produce a new rate, and your term is extended from that point. It can be useful if rates are rising and you want to lock in early, but the blended rate is often higher than what you'd get by simply waiting until renewal and negotiating. Ask your broker to model whether blend-and-extend or waiting to renewal is the better option in your specific situation.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Should I lock in for a longer or shorter term at renewal?">
  This depends on your rate expectations, financial stability, and plans for the property. A longer term (4–5 years) provides payment certainty and protects against rate increases, but costs more if rates fall. A shorter term (1–3 years) gives you more flexibility to renegotiate sooner, but exposes you to rate risk at the next renewal. In general, if current fixed rates are relatively low compared to historical averages, a 3–5 year fixed term offers good value. If rates are elevated, a 1–2 year term may be worth considering to benefit from anticipated rate reductions. Your broker can help model expected outcomes for each term option.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do I compare renewal offers from different lenders?">
  When comparing renewal offers, look beyond the headline rate. Evaluate the prepayment privileges (lump sum and payment increase allowances), portability provisions, refinancing flexibility, and any restrictions or limitations in the product. A mortgage with a slightly higher rate but 20% prepayment privileges and full portability may be a better long-term fit than the lowest-rate product with a no-frills structure. Use a broker to obtain quotes from multiple lenders on a comparable basis — they can present options in a side-by-side format so you can make an informed decision.
</Accordion>
