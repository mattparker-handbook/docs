If your down payment uses an RRSP Home Buyers’ Plan withdrawal or an FHSA qualifying withdrawal, involve Matt and your financial institution before moving funds. Eligibility and timing matter.

## Why the lender asks for it

The lender needs evidence of the account, the withdrawal and the funds available for closing. Your financial institution handles the withdrawal; the mortgage approval does not confirm tax eligibility.

## How to obtain it

1.  Ask Matt what account history and withdrawal evidence the lender requires. Download statements from the institution holding the RRSP or FHSA.
2.  For an RRSP Home Buyers’ Plan withdrawal, review CRA’s current eligibility guidance and obtain Form T1036. Complete the required portion and give it to the institution holding the RRSP as instructed.
3.  For an FHSA qualifying withdrawal, review CRA’s current guidance and obtain Form RC725. Follow the institution’s submission instructions; confirm the withdrawal qualifies before requesting it.
4.  Ask about investment-sale settlement and processing times. Retain withdrawal confirmations and receiving-account records showing the funds arrived. Discuss any uncertainty with your tax adviser before making a withdrawal.

## Before you upload

- Confirm each account belongs to the appropriate borrower and matches the withdrawal request.
- Keep the complete statement history and transfer trail requested by the lender.
- Do not assume the account balance is immediately available as cash.

## Common mix-ups

RRSP Home Buyers’ Plan and FHSA qualifying withdrawals have different conditions. A normal withdrawal may have different tax consequences. The lender’s acceptance of the down-payment source does not waive CRA rules or institution deadlines.

## If you are stuck

If closing is approaching or investments must be sold, tell Matt and the institution now. Confirm a workable timeline without relying on a guaranteed processing time.

Upload requested records through your application portal. Do not share your CRA or banking password, PIN, security answers or one-time codes. Ask Matt before redacting sensitive details; do not change figures, dates or transactions.
