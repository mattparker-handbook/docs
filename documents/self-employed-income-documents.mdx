Self-employed borrowers may need both personal tax records and business records. The correct package depends on how you operate and the mortgage program.

## Why the lender asks for it

The lender reviews income sustainability and how business results become your personal qualifying income. Revenue, business profit, salary and dividends are not interchangeable.

## How to obtain it

1.  Tell Matt whether you are a sole proprietor, partner or incorporated owner, and explain how you pay yourself. Obtain the file-specific document list before paying for new accounting work.
2.  Ask your accountant or tax preparer for complete filed personal T1 returns and relevant schedules for the requested years, along with your NOAs. Sole-proprietor business income commonly appears on T2125; rental schedules are different.
3.  For an incorporated business, ask for requested corporate financial statements, T2 filing records, ownership or incorporation records, and slips supporting salary or dividends where applicable.
4.  Download requested business bank statements from your bank and obtain current CRA balance evidence for the tax accounts specified. Discuss contracts, GST/HST records or interim statements only if requested.

## Before you upload

- Confirm the reporting years and include all pages and notes belonging to each requested statement.
- Identify whether financials are internally prepared, compiled, reviewed or audited; the lender decides what it accepts.
- Explain recent changes in ownership, revenue or business activity.

## Common mix-ups

CRA does not prepare your business bookkeeping financial statements. A blank T2125 is not your filed schedule. Two years is a common starting point, not a universal rule, and corporate profit is not automatically your personal mortgage income.

## If you are stuck

Forward the document list to your accountant and ask what is already available. Matt can clarify requirements before you commission additional work.

Upload requested records through your application portal. Do not share your CRA or banking password, PIN, security answers or one-time codes. Ask Matt before redacting sensitive details; do not change figures, dates or transactions.
