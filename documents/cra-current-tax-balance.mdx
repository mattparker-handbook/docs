A lender may need recent evidence of your CRA tax-account balance, particularly for self-employed income. An assessment notice and a current account statement serve different purposes.

## Why the lender asks for it

It helps the lender review outstanding tax obligations. A payment receipt shows a payment was made; it may not show that CRA has applied it or that the whole balance is cleared.

## How to obtain it

1.  Sign in to your CRA Individual account with full access and use the account-balance and statement-of-account service. Look for the information relevant to your personal income tax balance.
2.  Download or print the statement or account details requested by Matt, showing your identity, the account and current information. Ask which format the lender accepts if only an on-screen balance is available.
3.  If a payment was just made, retain the payment confirmation and check when it appears in CRA’s account records. For corporate balances, use the business account records requested rather than substituting personal records.

## Before you upload

- Make sure the record shows whose account it is and when the information applies.
- Disclose an amount owing or payment arrangement honestly.
- Ask if an updated balance statement is needed after a recent payment.

## Common mix-ups

A past NOA can show the amount owing when the return was assessed, not the balance today. Personal income tax, corporate income tax and GST/HST accounts are separate. Do not assume a payment arrangement satisfies the lender’s conditions.

## If you are stuck

If online records are unclear, contact CRA or your authorized accountant, then tell Matt what you can obtain. Do not send money solely to meet a guessed requirement; confirm the actual condition first.

Upload requested records through your application portal. Do not share your CRA or banking password, PIN, security answers or one-time codes. Ask Matt before redacting sensitive details; do not change figures, dates or transactions.
