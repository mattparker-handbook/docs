If you receive rental income or plan to rent part of a property, tell Matt whether it is existing, proposed, long-term or short-term income. Different evidence may be needed.

## Why the lender asks for it

The lender assesses how much rental income it can use and the property’s expenses. Receiving rent does not mean the full amount is automatically included in qualifying income.

## How to obtain it

1.  For an existing rental, ask your property manager or check your records for the complete current signed lease and any amendments.
2.  Download the requested account records showing rental deposits. Identify which property the rent relates to, without altering transactions.
3.  Ask your tax preparer for the filed rental-income schedule and tax records requested. Personal rental income commonly appears on T776, but corporate or other arrangements differ.
4.  For a proposed rental, ask Matt whether the lender needs an appraiser’s market-rent estimate or other evidence. Do not create a lease or arrange an appraisal yourself just to fill a guessed requirement.

## Before you upload

- Match the lease address, tenant, rent and dates to the property in the application.
- Disclose vacancies, concessions, related-party tenants and recent changes.
- Provide requested mortgage, tax, strata-fee and expense records for the rental property.

## Common mix-ups

A listing’s estimated rent is not an existing lease. Short-term booking revenue is not automatically treated like long-term rental income. A mortgage approval does not confirm a suite’s legal status or local rental compliance.

## If you are stuck

If there is no written lease or deposits arrive through a manager, explain that to Matt. We can ask what supporting evidence the lender will accept without inventing records.

Upload requested records through your application portal. Do not share your CRA or banking password, PIN, security answers or one-time codes. Ask Matt before redacting sensitive details; do not change figures, dates or transactions.
