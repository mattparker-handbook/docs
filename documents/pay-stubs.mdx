A pay stub or wage statement shows your earnings and deductions for a pay period, often with year-to-date totals. Your bank deposit usually shows only net pay.

## Why the lender asks for it

It helps verify current income and compare actual earnings with your employment letter. Variable pay may require more history than a single pay period.

## How to obtain it

1.  Sign in to your employer’s payroll service and open pay statements or pay history. Choose the pay periods in your personalized request.
2.  Download each complete statement as issued. If you receive paper statements, scan or photograph every page clearly.
3.  Ask payroll or HR for a replacement if the portal is unavailable, access ended after a job change or a statement is missing. Tell Matt if you have not yet received your first pay in a new role.

## Before you upload

- Show your name, employer, pay-period dates, gross and net pay, deductions and any year-to-date totals included on the statement.
- Provide records from each job requested.
- Flag unpaid leave, unusual bonuses or changes in hours so differences can be explained.

## Common mix-ups

A bank transaction is not a pay stub, and a T4 is not current payroll evidence. Do not multiply one unusually high pay period to estimate guaranteed annual income. Payroll systems differ, so there is no single download menu for every employer.

## If you are stuck

If payroll cannot supply the record promptly, let Matt know the employer and missing pay period. We can ask the lender which alternative evidence is acceptable.

Upload requested records through your application portal. Do not share your CRA or banking password, PIN, security answers or one-time codes. Ask Matt before redacting sensitive details; do not change figures, dates or transactions.
