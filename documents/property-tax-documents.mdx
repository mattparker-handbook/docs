A property-tax notice states the annual taxes charged. A current property-tax account statement or receipt may be needed separately to show payments and any balance owing.

## Why the lender asks for it

The lender may need the tax amount for affordability calculations and evidence of the property’s current tax status. An assessed property value is not proof of the tax bill or payment.

## How to obtain it

1.  For a municipal property, use your municipality’s property-tax account service or contact its tax office for the notice and any requested balance statement. Across Canada, the issuing authority and online service vary.
2.  For a BC rural property taxed by the province, use eTaxBC if enrolled or contact the provincial rural property-tax office. Do not assume eTaxBC holds municipal bills.
3.  If a payment has been made, obtain the receipt or updated account statement requested. Ask the tax authority how to obtain a current confirmation if a bank payment has not yet appeared.

## Before you upload

- Show the property address or identifier, tax year, annual amount and any balance or payment information requested.
- Disclose arrears, payment plans or tax deferral arrangements.
- For a new purchase, coordinate with your realtor and legal professional for seller documents and closing adjustments.

## Common mix-ups

A BC Assessment notice reports assessment information, not the annual tax bill. Monthly bank debits do not always prove the tax account is fully paid. Homeowner grants and tax deferral have separate conditions; do not assume they have been applied.
