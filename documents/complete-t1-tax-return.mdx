Your T1 is your personal Income Tax and Benefit Return. When a lender asks for the complete T1 General, a one-page income summary usually is not enough.

## Why the lender asks for it

The return and its schedules show how your income was reported, including business, rental and investment income. CRA’s assessment is a separate document.

## How to obtain it

1.  Ask the accountant or tax preparer who filed the return for the complete client copy as filed, including schedules and statements. Specify each requested tax year.
2.  If you filed yourself, open the relevant year in your tax software and look for the full return or filing-copy PDF, not just a tax summary. Menus differ by software.
3.  If you cannot retrieve it, consult CRA’s information-access guidance or contact CRA about the records available. Ask Matt before substituting a CRA online tax-return summary.

## Before you upload

- Include the complete requested package and all relevant schedules, such as business or rental statements.
- Confirm it is the final filed version, not an estimate or unfinished draft.
- Mention amended filings or reassessments so Matt can identify which records are needed.

## Common mix-ups

An NOA confirms CRA’s assessment, not the full information originally filed. A blank T1 downloaded from Canada.ca is not your return. Corporate T2 returns do not replace a shareholder’s personal T1.

## If you are stuck

Ask your preparer for “the complete filed T1 return and all schedules for \[year\], for a mortgage application.” Matt can clarify exactly what the lender is missing.

Upload requested records through your application portal. Do not share your CRA or banking password, PIN, security answers or one-time codes. Ask Matt before redacting sensitive details; do not change figures, dates or transactions.
