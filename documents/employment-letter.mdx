An employment letter confirms your current job and compensation. A clear letter often prevents a second request to your employer.

## Why the lender asks for it

The lender wants to verify that your employment and income match the application. It may also contact the employer using independently verified contact details.

## How to obtain it

1.  Ask Matt for the lender’s required details and date range before requesting the letter.
2.  Contact HR, payroll or your manager. Ask for a dated, signed employment confirmation on company letterhead, or the employer’s accepted official format.
3.  Ask it to address your legal name, role, start date, employment status and salary or hourly rate with guaranteed hours where applicable. Include the signer’s name, position and business contact details.
4.  For probation, leave, contract work or variable compensation, tell Matt first so the request reflects your situation. Ask the employer to correct omissions rather than editing the letter yourself.

## Before you upload

- Check your name, income and start date against the application and pay stubs.
- Distinguish guaranteed base earnings from overtime, bonuses and commissions.
- Use a recent letter meeting the actual request; a 30-day requirement is not universal.

## Common mix-ups

An offer letter describes an offer, not necessarily current employment. A self-written letter is not employer verification. Federal public-service employees may have a MyGCPay confirmation option; other employers use different systems.

## If you are stuck

You can ask: “I’m applying for a mortgage. Could you provide a current employment letter confirming my position, start date, employment status and guaranteed compensation, with a business contact for verification?” Matt can tailor this if needed.

Upload requested records through your application portal. Do not share your CRA or banking password, PIN, security answers or one-time codes. Ask Matt before redacting sensitive details; do not change figures, dates or transactions.
