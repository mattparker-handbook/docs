A void cheque or official pre-authorized debit banking form provides the account information needed to set up mortgage payments. It is not proof of your down payment.

## Why the lender asks for it

The lender or its servicing team needs accurate banking details and the appropriate payment authorization. A direct-deposit information sheet and a signed mortgage debit authorization may be separate requirements.

## How to obtain it

1.  Open your bank’s account-details or forms area and look for a void cheque or banking-information form. Download the bank-issued document, or ask your branch for one.
2.  If using a paper cheque, mark it VOID without obscuring the account details, following the lender’s instructions. Do not sign a blank cheque.
3.  Confirm which account can be used and whether a separate pre-authorized debit authorization must be signed. Provide it only through the verified channel requested by the lender or your application portal.

## Before you upload

- Check the account holder and institution, transit and account numbers carefully.
- Confirm this is the account you intend to use for payments and disclose if it is joint or belongs to someone else.
- Read the separate payment authorization before signing it.

## Common mix-ups

An e-transfer address is not mortgage payment-account information. Do not type account numbers into a general support message. Payment setup paperwork is not an instruction to send your closing funds to a new account.

## If you are stuck

If you cannot find the form, ask your bank for “official account information for mortgage pre-authorized payments.” Verify unexpected payment changes with the lender or legal professional using trusted contact details.

Upload requested records through your application portal. Do not share your CRA or banking password, PIN, security answers or one-time codes. Ask Matt before redacting sensitive details; do not change figures, dates or transactions.
