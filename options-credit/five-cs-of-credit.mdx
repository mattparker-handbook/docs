Your application is more than a credit score. The five Cs are a practical way to understand the lender’s wider review: capacity, character, credit, collateral and capital. They are a teaching framework, not a guaranteed approval formula.

## Capacity: can the payments be supported?

The lender reviews qualifying income, housing costs and other obligations. Gross Debt Service compares qualifying housing costs with gross income; Total Debt Service adds other debt obligations. Qualifying payments may be higher than your actual starting payment because of a stress test. Allowable ratios vary by lender, insurer and program—brochure ranges are not a universal entitlement.

- Disclose loans, credit lines, support payments and co-signed debt.
- Tell Matt which income is guaranteed and which is variable.
- Compare lender qualification with your own comfortable monthly budget.

## Character: the context around the numbers

Application consistency and past financial behaviour help a lender understand risk. Employment changes, moves or past financial difficulty do not tell the whole story on their own. Explain unusual circumstances honestly, with supporting records where available. This is not a judgement of your worth as a person.

## Credit: how you have managed borrowing

Lenders look at payment history, balances, credit limits, account age and significant adverse records, using more than one piece of information. The consumer score you see may differ from the score a lender uses. A strong score helps, but it does not replace verified income or acceptable property.

## Collateral: the property behind the loan

The lender considers value, condition, marketability and permitted use. A property can raise financing questions even when the borrower qualifies. An appraisal, strata review or other evidence may be requested. If the lender’s value differs from your purchase price, the down-payment plan may need adjusting.

## Capital: your own resources and buffer

Savings, investments and overall assets and liabilities help show your financial position. A documented gift may be acceptable, but the lender still verifies its source. A larger down payment can improve some applications; it cannot automatically cure every income, credit or property problem.

- Gather a complete picture of assets and debts.
- Keep a realistic emergency reserve where possible.
- Let Matt identify the weak spots early, before an offer deadline adds pressure.
