A Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) is a revolving credit facility secured against the equity in your property. Unlike a mortgage, which advances a lump sum that you repay over an amortization period, a HELOC allows you to draw funds up to a pre-approved limit, repay them, and draw again — on your schedule, for purposes you define. The interest rate is variable, the payments are typically interest-only on the drawn balance, and the credit room is secured by a registered charge on your home. For borrowers with meaningful equity, a HELOC is one of the most flexible and cost-effective financing tools available — but understanding how they are structured and regulated is essential before using one strategically.

## HELOC Basics

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Maximum Loan-to-Value Limits">
    For lenders regulated by OSFI (banks and federally regulated lenders), the rules are specific:
    - A **standalone HELOC** is capped at **65% LTV** — meaning your HELOC limit cannot exceed 65% of your property's appraised value.
    - When a HELOC is combined with a mortgage (a readvanceable structure), the **total combined LTV** — mortgage balance plus HELOC limit — cannot exceed **80%**.
    This means a borrower with a $1,000,000 property could have a mortgage + HELOC combined total of up to $800,000, with the HELOC component capped at $650,000 on a standalone basis.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Interest-Only Payments">
    You are only required to make interest payments on the amount you have actually drawn from the HELOC. If your limit is $200,000 but you've drawn $50,000, your minimum payment is interest on $50,000 only. There is no mandatory principal repayment schedule — though most financial planning strategies recommend paying down principal systematically.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Variable Rate Structure">
    HELOC rates are almost universally variable and expressed as a spread above the lender's prime rate. The most common pricing is **prime + 0.50%**, though some lenders price at prime + 1.00% or higher depending on the borrower profile. Rate movement tracks the Bank of Canada's overnight rate decisions in real time — HELOC costs rise and fall with rate changes.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Secured Against Your Property">
    A HELOC is not a personal line of credit. It is registered as a charge (typically a collateral charge) against your property's title. This means the lender has a legal claim on your home if you default on the HELOC, regardless of whether you also have a conventional mortgage on the same property. Understand the security registration structure before drawing significantly on a HELOC.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Two HELOC Structures

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Standalone HELOC">
    A standalone HELOC is a single revolving credit product with no mortgage component. It sits on its own as a registered charge against your property — up to the 65% LTV limit for OSFI-regulated lenders.

    **Best suited for:**
    - Borrowers who have fully paid off their mortgage and want revolving access to equity
    - Clients who want to separate their HELOC from their mortgage administratively
    - Property investors using equity from one property to fund activities on another

    **Key characteristics:**
    - Limit: up to 65% of appraised value (federally regulated lenders)
    - No mortgage component; interest-only revolving facility
    - Rate: prime + spread (variable)
    - Can be at the same institution as a mortgage or at a different institution (requires second charge or discharge)
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Readvanceable Mortgage (All-In-One)">
    A readvanceable mortgage combines a traditional amortizing mortgage with a HELOC under a single registered charge. The defining feature is that as you make principal payments on the mortgage, the HELOC room increases automatically — the mortgage and HELOC components are inversely linked within the 80% total LTV cap.

    **Bank-specific product names:**
    - TD: **Home Equity FlexLine**
    - RBC: **Homeline Plan**
    - Scotiabank: **STEP (Scotia Total Equity Plan)**
    - BMO: **ReadiLine**
    - CIBC: **Home Power Plan**
    - National Bank: **All-In-One**

    **Best suited for:**
    - Borrowers pursuing the Smith Manoeuvre
    - Clients who want a long-term revolving equity facility that grows as the mortgage is paid down
    - High-net-worth clients managing liquidity across multiple financial goals

    **Key characteristics:**
    - Total combined limit: up to 80% LTV
    - HELOC room grows automatically with each mortgage payment
    - Single registration — simplifies title and reduces legal costs vs. separate products
    - Available only at the Big Banks and a small number of credit unions
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Qualifying for a HELOC

<Steps>
  <Step title="Minimum 20% Equity">
    You must have at least 20% equity in your property to access a HELOC — the combined mortgage plus HELOC cannot exceed 80% LTV. For a property valued at $800,000, this means a maximum combined exposure of $640,000. If you purchased with less than 20% down and carry a high-ratio insured mortgage, you are not eligible for a HELOC until your equity reaches the 20% threshold.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Income and Credit Qualification">
    HELOC applications are subject to full income and credit qualification. The lender will assess your income, employment, existing debt obligations, and credit score in the same manner as a new mortgage application. Having equity is necessary but not sufficient — you must qualify on the income side as well.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stress Test on the HELOC Component">
    Under OSFI guidelines, the HELOC component of a readvanceable mortgage or standalone HELOC must be stress tested at **prime + 2%** (or the contract rate + 2%, whichever is higher) when the lender is federally regulated. This means the full HELOC limit — not just the drawn amount — factors into your qualifying debt service ratios.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Property Appraisal">
    An appraisal will typically be required to establish the current market value of your property, which determines your maximum allowable HELOC limit. In some cases, lenders will use an automated valuation model (AVM) for well-located properties with sufficient comparable sales data; in others, a full appraisal by a designated appraiser is required.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **The 65% standalone HELOC cap is widely misunderstood.** Many borrowers — and some advisors — assume a HELOC can be up to 80% of property value. It cannot, for a standalone HELOC at a federally regulated lender. The 80% cap applies only to the *combined* total of a mortgage plus HELOC in a readvanceable structure. A standalone HELOC is capped at 65% LTV, period. Confusing these two figures can lead to significant errors in financial planning and purchase structuring.
</Warning>

## Common HELOC Uses

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Renovations" icon="hammer">
    The most common HELOC use case. Interest-only draws allow homeowners to fund renovation projects without a fixed repayment schedule, repaying principal at their own pace.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Investment or Down Payment Bridge" icon="chart-line">
    Using home equity to fund a down payment on an investment property or as a bridge between purchase and sale closing dates. Requires careful cash flow planning given the variable rate.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Emergency Liquidity Fund" icon="shield">
    Establishing a HELOC while qualifying is easy (i.e., while employed, before retirement) and leaving it undrawn creates a low-cost emergency reserve. There are no fees on undrawn HELOC balances at most lenders.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Smith Manoeuvre" icon="arrow-right-arrow-left">
    A tax strategy where non-deductible mortgage debt is progressively converted to tax-deductible investment debt using a readvanceable HELOC. Requires a readvanceable mortgage — a standalone HELOC does not support this structure properly.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  **BC credit union HELOCs may offer higher LTV limits than federally regulated lenders.** Because BC credit unions are provincially regulated under BCFSA rather than OSFI, they are not bound by the 65% standalone HELOC cap. Some BC credit unions offer HELOC products at up to **80% LTV on a standalone basis** — a meaningful difference for borrowers seeking to maximize equity access. Confirm the specific terms, rate, and qualification requirements directly with the institution or through a mortgage broker.
</Note>
