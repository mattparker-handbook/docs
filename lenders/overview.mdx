Understanding who is lending you money matters as much as the rate they're offering. Canada's mortgage market is divided into three structurally distinct lender categories — the Big Six chartered banks, federally regulated monoline lenders, and provincially regulated BC credit unions — and each operates under a different set of rules, incentives, and product constraints. The differences between them affect everything from how your prepayment penalty is calculated to whether your income type qualifies, how your stress test is applied, and what happens when you need to break your mortgage early. For realtors, financial planners, and borrowers who care about the total cost of a mortgage — not just the rate on day one — understanding this landscape is essential.

## Summary Comparison

The table below provides a high-level snapshot of how the three lender categories compare across the policy areas that matter most. For a full, expanded breakdown of every policy dimension, see the [Policy Comparison Table](/lenders/policy-comparison-table).

| Feature | Big Banks | Monoline Lenders | BC Credit Unions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branch Access | Yes | No (broker only) | Yes (regional) |
| Rate Competitiveness | Moderate | Often best | Competitive |
| Prepayment Penalties | IRD (can be large) | IRD or 3-month interest | 3-month interest |
| Stress Test | OSFI B-20 applies | OSFI B-20 applies | Provincially regulated |
| Mortgage Portability | Yes | Usually yes | Varies |
| HELOC Products | Yes (readvanceable) | Limited | Yes |

<Note>
  Monoline lenders are **not directly accessible to the public**. They operate exclusively through the broker channel — meaning you must work with a licensed mortgage broker to access their products and rates. This is one of the most compelling reasons to engage a broker on any mortgage transaction: direct bank clients simply cannot access this segment of the market on their own.
</Note>
