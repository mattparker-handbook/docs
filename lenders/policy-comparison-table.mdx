This is your quick-reference master table for comparing lender categories across every policy dimension that matters in a BC mortgage transaction. Use it when evaluating which lender type to approach for a given client profile, when comparing renewal offers across institutions, or when advising clients on the trade-offs between lender types before they commit to a term. The detail pages linked throughout this section provide the full context behind each row — this table is the summary; the linked pages are the analysis.

## Master Policy Comparison

| Policy Area | Big Banks | Monoline Lenders | BC Credit Unions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stress Test** | OSFI B-20 — qualifying at contract rate + 2% or 5.25%, whichever is higher | OSFI B-20 — same as Big Banks | Provincially regulated MQR — modified rules, varies by institution |
| **Prepayment Penalty (Fixed Rate)** | IRD using posted rates — can be very large | IRD using contract rate — typically smaller | Usually 3-month interest |
| **Prepayment Penalty (Variable Rate)** | 3-month interest | 3-month interest | 3-month interest |
| **Annual Prepayment Privilege** | 10–20% of original balance (varies by bank) | 15–20% of original balance | 10–20% (varies by institution) |
| **Portability** | Yes — all six major banks | Usually yes — 60–90 day window | Varies by institution |
| **Assumability** | Yes, with lender approval and buyer qualification | Sometimes — lender and product dependent | Sometimes — confirm with institution |
| **Readvanceable HELOC** | Yes — all major banks offer proprietary products | Limited — First National Equityline is a notable exception | Yes — some credit unions offer readvanceable products |
| **Self-Employed / Stated Income** | Rarely — typically require 2 years NOA | Specialty monolines (Radius, Lendwise) offer alt programs | More flexible — many accept varied income documentation |
| **Rental Income Treatment** | 50–80% offset method (varies by bank) | 50–80% offset | Up to 100% at some credit unions |
| **New to Canada Programs** | Yes — RBC, Scotiabank, and TD have dedicated programs | Limited | Some — notably Khalsa CU and Vancity |
| **Max Amortization (Insured)** | 25 years; 30 years for new builds and first-time buyers | 25 years; 30 years for new builds and first-time buyers | 30–35 years at some credit unions |
| **Max Amortization (Conventional)** | 30 years (some lenders up to 35) | 30 years | Up to 35 years at some institutions |
| **HELOC Max LTV** | 65% standalone / 80% combined (OSFI cap) | 65% standalone / 80% combined | Up to 80% LTV standalone (provincial regulation) |
| **Branch / In-Person Access** | Yes — national branch networks | No — broker channel only | Yes — regional branches |
| **Rate Competitiveness** | Moderate — rarely the lowest | Often the most competitive on standard deals | Competitive; strongest value on complex files |

## Choosing the Right Lender for the Situation

<Note>
  **Best rate on a straightforward deal → Monoline Lender**

  For a borrower with T4 employment income, strong credit, a clean property, and no complex income considerations, a monoline lender — accessed through a mortgage broker — will almost always offer the most competitive rate in the market. Lower overhead, institutional funding, and broker-channel efficiency combine to produce pricing that the Big Banks and credit unions rarely match on vanilla files.
</Note>

<Note>
  **Readvanceable HELOC required → Big Bank**

  If a client's financial plan requires a readvanceable mortgage — particularly for the Smith Manoeuvre, long-term equity management, or an all-in-one banking relationship — a Big Bank is the practical choice. The TD Home Equity FlexLine, RBC Homeline Plan, Scotiabank STEP, and equivalent products at BMO, CIBC, and National Bank are the only widely available readvanceable structures in the market. Monolines and credit unions offer limited equivalents.
</Note>

<Tip>
  **Client didn't pass the OSFI B-20 stress test → BC Credit Union**

  Before recommending any alternative or private lending solution, explore BC credit unions. Provincially regulated institutions are not bound by OSFI B-20 in the same manner as banks and monolines, and many apply a modified qualifying rate that allows clients to qualify at a lower threshold. For clients who narrowly miss the federal stress test — by 5–10% of qualifying income — a credit union is often the cleanest, most affordable path forward.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  **Complex income, self-employed, or non-traditional file → Credit Union or Alt Monoline**

  Clients with incorporated business income, multiple income streams, foreign income, recent self-employment, or non-standard documentation are poorly served by Big Bank underwriting. BC credit unions like First West and Vancity are experienced with complex income files and approach them with significantly more flexibility. If the credit union route is exhausted, specialty monoline lenders (Radius Financial, Lendwise) operate in the alternative segment with purpose-built programs for this profile — at higher rates, but with legitimate qualifying paths.
</Tip>
